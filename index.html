The Trail of Tears refers to the of several Native American nations from their lands in the southeastern United States during the 1830s and 1840s. After the was signed in 1830, thousands of , Choctaw, Chickasaw, Muscogee and Seminole people move west to Indian Territory, mainly in present-day Oklahoma. This of population was one of the saddest episodes in the history of the Native Americans.

At a historical level, it was estimated that between 60,000 and 100,000 people were relocated . They were forced to leave everything behind and take only their basic . Entire families were forced to migrate, travelling up to about 800 miles (1,300 km) for several months before reaching Indian Territory. The different routes combined stretched over more than 5,000 miles (8,100 km). The conditions were extremely difficult, forcing them to travel in winter, sometimes barefoot, with only a blanket to warm themselves. The procession was escorted by who wanted to make sure that the people would go where they were asked to.

Those who used to move freely from one place to another without knowing anything about the notion of or frontiers were now experiencing them the hard way. A lot of them died of extreme exhaustion, cold and . It was estimated that between 13,200 and 16,700 people died during the relocation and 245 million acres of were confiscated, while the was never fully paid.
